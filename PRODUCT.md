# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Primary users are engineering recruiters, hiring managers, technical interviewers, peers, and future collaborators who want to understand Tiew Kee Hui's engineering judgment quickly. Secondary users are readers arriving from search or shared links for specific technical notes.

Visitors usually have limited time. Recruiters need role, location, credibility, resume, and contact paths. Engineering readers need clear article titles, accurate summaries, readable code, and enough surrounding context to trust the material.

## Product Purpose

This site is a software engineer portfolio and technical blog. It should communicate senior engineering capability through work history, technical focus, writing quality, and durable project artifacts.

Success means a first-time visitor can understand who Kee Hui is, what kind of engineering problems he works on, and where to go next within the first screen. Long-form readers should be able to read technical posts comfortably without the portfolio layer becoming a distraction.

## Brand Personality

Precise, practical, systems-minded.

The site should feel like a clean engineering workbench: technical without terminal cosplay, personal without becoming informal, and memorable without sacrificing reading comfort.

## Anti-references

Avoid generic developer portfolio templates with oversized hero slogans, repeated icon cards, vague claims, and decorative code snippets. Avoid novelty desktop UI that traps users, hides normal navigation, or makes articles hard to read. Avoid startup landing-page exaggeration, dark-blue developer sameness, and blog layouts that bury the author's credibility behind a chronological archive.

## Design Principles

1. Show proof before polish. Experience, systems, metrics, projects, and writing should carry credibility more than visual decoration.
2. Keep reading sacred. Blog posts need direct URLs, semantic markup, calm typography, fast loading, and minimal interface interference.
3. Make the portfolio scannable. Recruiters and technical visitors should find role, location, stack, recent work, resume, GitHub, LinkedIn, and contact without hunting.
4. Use the OS metaphor as structure, not theater. Workbench mode should organize content and create personality while Reader mode remains the reliable baseline.
5. Preserve maintainability. Content should remain markdown-first, Astro routes should stay crawlable, and visual ideas should not require a fragile client-side app to read a post.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA for core pages and components. Reader mode is the accessibility baseline. Workbench mode must preserve semantic sections, links, buttons, focus states, keyboard access, visible exits, reduced-motion support, and direct page routes.

The design should support color-blind users through text, structure, and visible states rather than color alone. Motion should be restrained and disabled under `prefers-reduced-motion`.
